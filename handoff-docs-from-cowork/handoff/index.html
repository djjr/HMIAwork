HANDOFF — Alignment Talks & LYSK Social Science Unit

Session date: 2026-07-14. Project context: Human and Machine Intelligence Alignment (Dan's course design).

What this session produced

1. Three "Russell-analog" talks (full texts in alignment_talks.md). Starting from Stuart Russell's 2015 value-alignment talk (paperclips, King Midas, Rutherford/Szilard, fusion-containment, inverse RL), we composed historical analogues, regressing in time:

  • The corporation talk (~1620 London): chartered joint-stock companies as "artificial persons" — charter = objective function, VOC/Banda atrocity = paperclip maximizer, Coke's soulless immortal person = superintelligence, gunpowder containment = fusion containment, "read the common law then ask Parliament" = IRL + extended conversation.
  • The experts talk (Neolithic fireside): epistemic asymmetry — unverifiable expertise as the alignment problem; community can only evaluate surface signals, which experts optimize. Elder's four rules = ancestors of peer review, registered trials, falsifiability, conflict-of-interest law. "Smoking rack and the debt" plays the Szilard role (food storage breaks the immediate-return economy → holdable surplus → debt → hierarchy; Woodburn, Graeber).
  • The other-minds talk (earliest humans): mutual opacity among matched intelligences — the Hobbesian trap / Schelling's reciprocal fear of surprise attack. Solutions: commensality (costly signals), public plan-declaration, gossip (distributed reputation), synchronized ritual (common knowledge), marriage exchange (interest entanglement). Key point: nobody solves interpretability — opacity stays; institutions make hidden intentions irrelevant via incentives.

Shared epilogue theme across all three: every historical solution assumed agent and overseer matched in speed and power, so deception and detection co-evolved. AI breaks exactly that assumption.

2. Student abstraction model answer (in alignment_talks.md): all four talks = delegation under opacity — capturing gains from an optimizer whose objectives you can't fully specify and whose inner state you can't inspect. Institutions (law, peer review, reputation, ritual) are the fossil record of prior solutions.

3. LYSK Social Science unit ("Least You Should Know") — instructor guide finished as LYSK_instructor_guide.docx (one page, verified). Six-movement arc:

  1. The puzzle — problem of social order; collective action; coordination vs. cooperation (Hobbes, Olson, Schelling)
  2. Groups & norms — norms as group property; reputation, costly signals, commons (Axelrod, Ostrom, Durkheim). Limit: doesn't scale
  3. Markets — prices, knowledge problem, Pareto; limits: externalities, public goods (Smith, Hayek, Pareto)
  4. Hierarchy & its control — Coase, Weber, principal–agent, Goodhart, Scott; PLUS social control of organizations (charters, regulation) and of experts (licensure, codes — Freidson's professionalism as "third logic")
  5. Aggregation — Arrow/Condorcet, Schelling micromotives, Hirschman exit/voice, Marx (whose order?)
  6. Selves — Mead (I/me, generalized other = "the group internalized" — the norms→selves hinge), Goffman presentation of self, socialization, identity; closes groups→selves→groups loop

Pedagogical design: run the four talks first as narrative; LYSK is the formal debrief — concepts land as recognition. Capstone assessment prompt: "which mechanisms quietly assume agent and overseer are matched in speed and power — and what breaks if they aren't?"
