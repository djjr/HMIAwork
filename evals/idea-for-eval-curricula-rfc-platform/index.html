Evals › Idea for Eval Curricula RFC Platform

What if we used PubPub site as follows.

The two object types A course proposal is a standalone document with its own ID, version history, and comment thread: outcomes, prerequisites, modules or weeks, assessment. A curriculum plan is also standalone, but its course list is a mix of two kinds of entries: references to course proposals at a specific version, and inline stubs (a title plus a paragraph) for courses nobody has written up yet. A purely conceptual curriculum is just a plan whose course list is all stubs.

The three relationships Reference, pinned. When Plan A says "uses Evaluation Science @v2," that reference doesn't move on its own. If the course's author publishes v3, the plan's maintainers get a notification and choose whether to bump. This is the same discipline as dependency pinning in software, and it's what lets two institutions share a course while disagreeing about which version they mean. The review thread on the plan stays coherent because the plan's content doesn't change underneath its reviewers.

Promote. A stub inside a plan gets pulled out into its own course proposal (with attribution to the plan it came from), and the plan's entry becomes a reference. This is how the course repository grows organically: people write whole curricula, and the courses that attract interest get fleshed out.

Fork. Someone takes Evaluation Science v2 and adapts it for their context. The result is a new course proposal recording "derived from Eval science v2," so the lineage is visible and reviewers can ask "why did you diverge here?" Forks don't merge back automatically; if the adaptation is good, the original author can adopt it in their next version.

Review workflow, same for both types Submit from the template, get an ID, enter a review round with a close date. Reviewers leave anchored comments plus one summary review. Author responds and publishes a new version; the thread carries over. Status moves through draft, in review, revised, and then accepted, withdrawn, or superseded. Accepted doesn't mean "official," just "reviewed and stable enough for others to reference."

Two rules that keep this from getting messy Route comments to the right object. A comment on the course itself ("week 4 is too thin") belongs on the course thread, where all its users benefit. A comment on the course's role in a plan ("this doesn't belong before the ethics module") belongs on the plan thread. When a reviewer is reading a plan and drills into a referenced course, the UI should make clear which thread they're writing in. This is the piece most tools get wrong, and it's the one worth thinking about before choosing a platform.

Versions are immutable once cited. Once any plan references v2, v2 is frozen. Corrections go in v3. Otherwise a plan's reviewers can't trust what they reviewed.

In implementation terms, a plan's course list is just a YAML-ish block in the Markdown front matter (courses: [{id: C-012, version: 2}, {stub: "Capstone", ...}]), which a static-site build resolves into links, and which PubPub's collections model handles natively if you go that route.
