Aiden: Sacred texts exist because society can survive only if people agree with each other on the fundamental principles of how life should be lived. If kept as an oral tradition, these principles could shift and stray over time, leading to the degradation of a well-functioning society. Sacred texts generally serve to "align" everyone in a population and reinforce collaboration and harmony between peoples. Sacred texts from all ideologies and societies frequently mention honesty, compassion, and fairness. Many sacred texts also cover repentance and forgiveness for when a person messes up. Something from my own upbringing that would qualify as a sacred text is a principle that many Americans don't even think about: that we should tolerate even those who have differing opinions from us. Free speech in America is an extraordinary thing, especially when comparing it to authoritarian countries in Africa, the Middle East, and Asia. We have become so accustomed to hearing arguments about policy and values online and in person that we don't even stop and think about how much of a blessing it is that we can have those conversations in the first place. Free speech/tolerance of others is a major sacred text in my upbringing and the upbringing of all Americans, as it sets the standard that we all have the right to disagree with one another freely.

Arya: "Sacred texts" refer to widely recognized works that capture some sort of consensus that a culture or community arrives at. Sacred texts are often unimpeachable or beyond reproach, and someone who outright rejects a sacred text is considered suspect or an outcast. The generic purpose is to create a shared, baseline moral system that can be enforced, rather than individual agents choosing to act completely independently. Typically, sacred texts suggest that humans should act for the communal gain, rather than solely for selfish reasons.

Something like classroom rules from elementary school could qualify as sacred texts, as they were widely accepted as the baseline expectations for behavior, and students who did not adhere to them were disciplined. This contributed to aligning the students towards creating an environment conducive to learning together, rather than students doing whatever they wished.

Weixiao: Why sacred texts:

The original function of “sacred texts were enlightening human intelligence with generic advice on moral standards, social rules and philosophy (i.e. altruism), especially at times when the existing experience and rules no longer match with the increasing need of intelligence led by development/expansion. Throughout the time, institutions or communities record and collect the useful texts and re-interpret them as general inspiration to the latest world. The sacred texts are not necessarily proper to strictly follow; to understand the intelligence in the texts, it is better to consider the literary contexts and learn the mindset of the authors beyond the rigid words. 

Personal example: 

There's a very popular motto in China: “Treat your aged kin as the elderly should be treated, and then extend that to the treatment of the aged kinsmen of others; treat your young kin as the young should be treated, and then extend it to the young children of others." The motto was often used to encourage people helping elders and children around them; I learned it from my parents at around 4.

However, the full text (from my middle school text book) was like this:

"Treat your aged kin as the elderly should be treated, and then extend that to the treatment of the aged kinsmen of others; treat your young kin as the young should be treated, and then extend it to the young children of others.
