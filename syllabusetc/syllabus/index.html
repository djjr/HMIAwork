Course Description

The challenge of ensuring that the actions of individuals and systems — whether human or machine — are consistent with shared goals, reflect our values, and promote societal well-being is known as 'the alignment problem.' Over millennia, humans have developed many coordination and cooperation 'technologies' — such as customs, values, norms, laws, organizations, governments, and markets—that partially solve the problem of human intelligence alignment.As we develop and deploy advanced technologies like artificial intelligence, we are similarly concerned that their use is consistent with shared goals, reflect our values, and promote societal well-being. In this course we will explore the parallels between human intelligence alignment and machine intelligence alignment to help engineers and technologists become reflective practitioners who can grapple wisely with the alignment problem broadly understood.

Character or Structure?

11.12 | Qualification Alignment among agents is obstructed by uncertainty about competence and trustworthiness before authority is granted; you can't fully verify in advance whether someone or something is capable of doing what they claim, or will act responsibly once entrusted with the power to act. Qualification mechanisms counter that by gatekeeping: credentials, licensing, certification exams, and capability evaluations that sort who is fit to act from who is not before authority changes hands rather than after harm is done. This session will include lightning talks by class members, lecture, and, optionally, an in-class exercise. SLIDES

Readings

11.17 | Safety No system, however carefully specified, behaves reliably in every circumstance; components fail, environments drift outside the range they were built for, and often the first sign of trouble is the failure itself. Safety mechanisms counter that by building margin into the system in advance: redundancy so no single point of failure is fatal, fail-safes that arrest the system when it drifts outside safe bounds, interpretability tools that let you inspect what's happening inside before it acts, and robustness testing that deliberately stresses the system before the world does it involuntarily. This session will include lightning talks by class members, lecture, and, optionally, an in-class exercise. SLIDES

Readings

  • Levenson and Turner An Investigation of the Therac-25 Accidents
  • Khlaaf 2023 Toward_comprehensive_risk_assessments

11.19 | Transparency Alignment among agents is obstructed by the opacity of inner states and of the world; you can't see what someone else is thinking, and even your own memory is fallible. Transparency and record keeping mechanisms counter that by manufacturing external traces of cognition and action that other people (or systems) can inspect, verify, and learn from. This session will include lightning talks by class members, lecture, and, optionally, an in-class exercise. SLIDES

12.01 | Structures Alignment among agents can be obstructed by the concentration of power in any single actor, human or machine, who then has both the means and the motive to act against everyone else's interests. Structural mechanisms counter that by manufacturing distributed authority: separation of powers, checks and balances, term limits, third-party monitoring, and democratic accountability, each designed so that no actor's self-interest can operate without another actor's independent self-interest to resist it. This session will include lightning talks by class members, lecture, and, optionally, an in-class exercise. SLIDES

12.03 | Control and Oversight Alignment among agents can be obstructed by the risk that an agent already granted scope to act may misuse it before we notice or can respond. Control mechanisms put limits on what can be done and empower other agents to stop it: governance structures and role-based permissions bound authority before it's exercised, sandboxing and access control that contain what a system can reach, and kill switches, whistleblowing, and investigative oversight let someone outside system halt or expose it when something has gone wrong. This session will include lightning talks by class members, lecture, and, optionally, an in-class exercise. SLIDES

12.08 | Governance Every mechanism we have met in this course, from qualification to control and oversight, whether in connection with humans, organizations, experts, or machines, had to be designed, argued over, funded, staffed, and eventually enforced or ignored by someone. That someone is a field: governance. Of of particular interest to us, AI Governance. It spans policy researchers, standards bodies, safety teams inside labs, regulators, auditors, and international coordination efforts, all working the same underlying problem asking which known or still to be invented governance practices will be effective and implementable against which alignment problems. This session steps back from the mechanisms to look at the people and institutions building them, what the field does, what paths lead into it, and where its live open problems are.
