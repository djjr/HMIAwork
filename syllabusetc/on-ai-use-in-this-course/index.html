SyllabusEtc › On AI Use in this Course

I recommend heavy use of AI chatbots as a contributor to your learning. I forbid the use of AI chatbots to produce work that you ask me to respond to as if it were your own. Don't make me waste time evaluating LLM output. It will not make me happy.

The purpose of every task I assign you in this course is to allow you to master a set of ideas and skills. The tasks are meant as workouts for your mind. Using Claude or Gemini or ChatGPT to generate the answer to an essay question makes about as much sense as bringing a hydraulic lift to the weight room at the gym. Outsourcing your physical exercise to a machine during your workouts would be absurd. And using a machine - say an electric motor - in a bike race would be unsportsmanlike, unethical, and would likely get you disqualified. It would also be insulting to teammates, opponents, fans, and officials. Keep this analogy in mind.

I expect you to have intense conversations with LLMs about ideas. "Help me understand what an impact regularizer as discussed in Amodei et al 2016 is." "OK, let me see if I can put this into my own words ... blah blah blah." "So, the attention matrices are basically doing a projection of the input into a subspace of the embedding space?" Even, "here's my summary of X, what am I missing?" In every case, treat it like you will have an oral exam on the topic the next day. The only goal is to master the concept, acquire the skill.

When we submit essays or make presentations we will not treat LLMs as reference materials. If the LLM says "the point you made resonates with that made by Ryan (2019)" you do not just drop in a reference to Ryan (2019) unless you go and read Ryan (2019) and verify that it supports the point you made. It's great that ChatGPT turned you on to this source and made the connection but it's up to you to make something of this hint.

Bottom lines. Lying is wrong. Putting your name on as author if you were not the actual author is lying. Eating vegetables is hard. Lifting weights is hard. Running ten miles is hard. Eating sugar is easy. Lifting with a forklift or driving ten miles is easy. Eating vegetables, lifting weights, and running are good for you. Eating sugar and not exercising is bad for you. Having an LLM do the hard parts is like eating sugar and not exercising.

Pragmatics

If we use text composed by an LLM in something we produce to submit for this course we will change the text color to purple.

If we've engaged with an LLM like a colleague we will either make it a coauthor with description of contributions. For example:

  • This is a Claude Sonnet produced document edited by Dan Ryan.
  • My ideas in this document were strongly influenced by exchanges with Claude Sonnet.

When you honestly use the LLM to learn something, you might not need to cite it. But don't submit something you still don't understand. Always expect to have to explain anything you submit in writing. If the story is something like "The second half of my answer is something I didn't understand until Claude explained it to me (multiple times). I do understand it now." then we don't need a citation.

All this seems tedious and pedantic. But we are living in a transition and you are a student. Your goal should be learning, the development of your mind, the transformation of your perspective, the acquisition of skills. Those are hard work. There is a constant temptation to reward hack. One of the reasons we are in school rather than just being autodidacts is because teachers can play the role of keeping the learning honest. That's part of my job. That's what this is about.
