#51

Last year we did not explicitly say "constitution," relying instead on the category "Structures and Institutional Design." Interestingly, that material really was about structures and not character which seems a big element in AI constitutions.

Let's start generically - across our four domains, one approach to alignment is to write down some foundational ideas and treat them as a fixed reference. Necessary properties are general enough to apply to novel situations, firm enough to not drift, etc. People do it with ground rules (and religious edicts, holy books, covenants), organizations do it with mission statements and policy books, experts have codes and such.

Structures vs character theme throughout.

Connecting back, a constitution is an attempt to manufacture and materialize the generalized other. It tells you how the world will respond. It's the normative handbook.

Structures or Character?

Publius1

1 This is the pseudonym under which John Jay, James Madison, and Alexander Hamilton published the Federalist Papers.
writes in Federalist #51: "If men were angels, no government would be necessary. If angels were to govern men, neither external nor internal controls on government would be necessary. In framing a government which is to be administered by men over men, the great difficulty lies in this: you must first enable the government to control the governed; and in the next place oblige it to control itself." The argument is that government exists only because people aren't angels, and the design problem is to build a structure that stays stable even though the people running it will act in self-interest rather than virtue. For Madison it is a mechanism-design puzzle: give the government enough power to control the governed, but then separately force it to control itself; external checks like elections won't do that job alone.

The solution is architectural, not moral. The authors envision each part/branch of government having an incentive to resist encroachment by the others. "Ambition counteracts ambition" - self interest (which we might think of as a vice) supplies mutual social control (yielding social order which we might think of as a virtue).

Course Connections. We'll want to think about how this relates to organizations and hierarchy which are less homeostatic in spirit. And we might think about it in connection with markets where a similar reliance on self-interest drives the system toward alignment.
