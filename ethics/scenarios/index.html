Ethics › Scenarios

I am S. I am a young (fresh out of JHU with a bachelors in CS) software engineer at a small company that makes routing and tracking software. It’s end-of-quarter crunch time, and the CTO just announced bonuses tied to “feature velocity.” Ravi, my senior teammate and assigned mentor, is frantically pushing code to make the analytics dashboard live before Friday.

I'm in the position of approving things in preliminary code reviews. In reviewing his commit, I saw a logging function that tracks drivers’ off-route stops - including lunch breaks and personal errands, geo-tagged with businesses entered, anything a driver does between start of day and end of day - quite a bit of data no one told them would be collected. When I asked about it, Ravi laughed. “Yeah, management wanted more behavioral data. It’s anonymized—no big deal, no harm, no foul. Just approve it. They’re tracking performance trends, not people.” The dashboard shows that this feature alone could boost route optimization by 4% and those are the kinds of numbers that the clients care about. And I knew how Ravi really went above and beyond to get things ready to launch on time.

Changing this feature would involve a numerous levels of approvals and you wonder if this is the only place in the code where we've done something like this. It could be a big hairball in terms of fixes. And heads could roll. It might not be pretty. Ravi's totally right that nothing nefarious is designed in by our team. But a part of my brain was thinking if drivers were to turn off the data collection that would completely trash our product.

The Slack channel is full of celebratory emojis for “record deployment speed.” My finger twitches as my cursor hovers over “Approve.” Everyone’s racing ahead, I thought. Does anyone notice where the data trail leads? Isn't this exactly what we were talking about at the company offsite when Sam, the CEO boasted about how we cared about our actual users?

I pause and realize that this must be one of those ethical situations I learned about in school. I try to recall the things I wasn't paying attention to.

I am N. I am a young (fresh out of JHU with a bachelors in CS) software engineer at a small company that makes health apps. I'm working on UI/UX aspects of one of our products a web-based health assistant. We have a lot of testing data showing that signups are abandoned at a very high rate at the informed consent screen where we explain what data we collect and how it will be shared. My colleagues and I have run a boat load of tests to see what tweaks can affect the conversion rate and we repeatedly find that "less is more" - including fewer details, making the print smaller, requiring scrolling down, using less contrasting colors for text, etc. - they all improve the rate at which people move on to the next screen. And if people don't get past this step in the sign up, they never get to take advantage of the real health benefits and cost savings that our app provides. We are getting close to having to freeze the next version release so some decisions are going to need to be made. I feel like there's been some drift on our team away from the "first respect the customer" maxim that is painted on the wall at our offices. Even some of the privacy mavens seem to be fine with reducing how informed the informed consent is. I can feel my naivete on display whenever I try to bring this up - it's like I'm the only one that doesn't get how a production environment is where the "rubber meets the road" and it's not a theoretical discussion in an ethics class. But I think back to those classes that I took that mentioned "professional ethics" - is this the very kind of thing they were about? It seems so much smaller than bridges falling down. Maybe I'm making too much of this, but it kind of keeps me up at night. I try to recall the things in those classes that I wasn't paying enough attention to. Maybe I should confront the team? Talk to my manager? Or their manager? I'm just not sure.
