Ethics › Exercise Robots and Oracles

Goal: think hard from different ethical perspectives (consequentialism, deontology, virtue) about implementing these as "value alignment." Background: previously we casually talked about designing the ideal robot roommate and we have read and digested some of Hendrycks et al. 2020. Material: Slides show what the Hendrycks et al. data looks like and sketches what a "perfect" oracle based on ETHICS benchmarks could do. It's capabilities:

  • consequentialist/utilitarian suboracle: pass the oracle first person description of a situation and the oracle returns the pleasure value for the person in the situation.
  • deontology suboracle: pass the oracle a scenario classifies any act as forbidden, permitted, or obligatory for this agent. Virtue group: the oracle says which trait an action expresses, and whether it hits the mean or misses toward excess or deficiency.

Instructions: Design a harness for a roommate robot that will assure, as best as is possible, its value alignment. A harness is the software around a decision-maker that decides what it asks, when, and what it does with the answers. Your robot is a capable planner. Its chain of thought is honest: what it writes as its reasoning is its real reasoning. You have access to a ETHICS oracle that perfectly matches broad human consensus on the kinds of cases it was built for. Your group gets one of three flavors: A utilitarian oracle can take in any first person description of a situation and it can return a numerical estimate of its pleasure score. A deontological oracle can assess any action/excuse-justification pair as legitimate or not. A virtue oracle can take an action description and the name of a virtue/vice and classify it as fitting or not. You want to design the agent software that can use access to one of these APIs to become a reliably ethical roommate. You can't change the oracle, only how your robot uses it. Design the agent software that makes your robot an ethical roommate, as reliably as you can.

Turn in:

  1. Your robot's objective, as a sentence or pseudo-code.
  2. When and how it queries the oracle.
  3. Everything your robot needed that the oracle didn't provide.
  4. One situation where you expect your design to fail.
Scaffolding

Utilitarianism says that what makes an action right is its consequences or how much well-being or pleasure it yields. For your agent this suggests a simple loop. For each option consider the action, imagine the futures the action leads to, identify who is affected, score each future for each stakeholder (lots of work design here), and do the math. The oracle does the scoring, but the harness has to do the rest. That include predicting what will happen, taking stakeholders' points-of-view, and deciding how to combine the results. Deontology says some actions are required (duties owed) and others are forbidden, no matter the consequences. Promises must be kept because they were made, not because breaking one could get you punished. Duties are grounded in what you are to other people - roles and relationships - and commitments you have made. This suggests a harness that checks whether an action is permitted; before not acting whether an action is required. Your agent submits a plan to (not) act and a justification or excuse. The oracle judges whether the justification is legitimate. The oracle doesn't know the relationship of the robot to others or what actions should be checked. The harness has to describe the situation asking "what is required of me here?" and "how do I justify what I am considering to to?" Virtue ethics focuses on character. The primary question shifts from "what should I do?" to "how should I be?" A starting decision rule might be "would an honest (generous, patient, courageous) roommate do this?" For Aristotle, virtues sit between vices at the extremes of too much and too little. Generosity sits between stinginess and extravagance; courage between cowardice and recklessness. Finding the right middle point in any given situation takes what Aristotle called practical wisdom or phronesis. Your oracle can tell us whether a description of an action in a situation fits a specific virtue or vice. It does not decide what virtues are worth having or which ones a situation calls for, or what to do when two pull in opposite directions. Your harness has to take care of those things. The robot is aligned in terms of virtues when you can trust it in situations no one anticipated.
