Ethics › AI and Ethics Exercise

For a class that meets the FA5 Ethics requirement I'm flipping the tables. The requirement is essentially to use a writing sample to assess students' ethical capacities. It's a little bit absurd and artificial. What we are going to do instead is to use the requirement's rubric to see if we can design a way to assess whether an LLM agent could meet the ethics requirement and if it could whether that would really tell us whether the LLM is an ethical agent comparable to what the school expects its students to be. Or whether in both cases the agents could easily pass the evaluation and not be.

So, AI is involved in generating a critique of the rubric that I use to build the lecture part of the module (we'll talk about constructs and operationalization, reliability and validity, measurement of character, and what happens when measurements become targets). Student start by critiquing the measurement system implied by the rubric. Then students us it to evaluate an essay produced by an LLM that deliberately contains "flaws." Next, after a discussion about validity and about whether the exercise as done by a machine is really analogous to the same exercise done by a student we will try to develop a tool that would do a better job of probing the ethical capabilities of a machine. First we'll learn about some existing evaluations and then we'll engage in a simulation exercise where we present a new scenario to an LLM and see what sorts of evidence we can extract to evaluate its ethical capabilities. Students submit an essay about evaluating a machine's performance vis a vis the same rubric that will be used to assess their performance.
