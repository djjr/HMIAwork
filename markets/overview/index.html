Markets › Overview

Markets

The human-intelligence alignment problem is rooted in divergent interests, autonomous agency, and the irreducible opacity of other minds.

OR

Alignment problems arise when autonomous agents with partially conflicting interests must coordinate under conditions of limited mutual knowledge.

This also gives markets a natural role:

  • divergent interests → incentives and exchange;
  • autonomy → choice, competition, and exit;
  • opacity → prices, reputation, monitoring, and signaling.

The mechanisms help, but never eliminate the underlying problem. Even perfectly aligned interests can produce coordination failures when agents cannot observe one another or predict the equilibrium. A market is a decentralized mechanism in which strategically responsive agents with partially conflicting interests coordinate through the information and incentive effects generated by one another’s choices. Both alignment and social order are the problem of coordinating agents with partially conflicting goals, distributed information, and interdependent actions. Shared meaning (thinking alike, understanding one another's reward functions) and hierarchy (giving some agents coercive power over other agents) are two families of alignment mechanisms. If a collective sense of "the good" is a part of shared meaning or the driving force for hierarchy coordination and cooperation may emerge.

Markets are a family of decentralized feedback-based mechanisms for alignment, but they align behavior around signals and incentives that may only be imperfect proxies for "the good."

In our previous sessions on shared meaning we hypothesized alignment is facilitated when agents ave "on the same page, coordinate and cooperate because they partially over come the problem of not Knowing what is in the other agents' heads By definition socially constructed reality is shared reality. The more socally constructed reality we have the more we can predict what one another will do and the more we can correctly interpret what one another mean.

Individual humans grow up in shared environments and as a result have similar world models that include their peers having similar world models. In organizations we invest heavily in individuals participating as "office holders" rather than unique individuals. When we create and recognize socially sanctioned experts we train them as generic channelers of accumulated social knowledge. And we tame (align) machine intelligence by making it acquire a model of how we think and what we care about.

The basic idea in all of this is that if agents share common meanings then the problem of social order and alignment are solved.

A strong argument can be made that shared meaning is NECESSARY for cooperation and coordination, but we might be on weaker ground if we are claiming that it is SUFFICIENT.

People can share meaning but still pursue their own selfish interest.

And historically it has not been perceived to be enough. Most cultures introduce hierarchy to complement shared meaning.

Hobbes actually locates the source of the problem in shared meaning. People are similar and so often want the same things and when there's not enough of the same thing to go around people become enemies. When everyone is free to do as they please, we spend too much time fighting and too little time building things and growing food. We want is public goods like peace and security, but just agreeing to live in peace is a fragile arrangement.

Hobbes' solution is the emergence of a coercive force that can enforce agreements.

In our first ancient alignment vignette, the elder suggests all agreements be made in front of the whole village so as to avoid my-word-against-yours dilemmas.

That solution, though, is not scalable. And it rests on a foundation of shared meaning - that the threat of everyone knowing you are a liar or a cheat would prevent you from being a liar and a cheat. That's a fragile system again. Unless it's backed up by a credible threat of consequences such as none of those assembled will help you out next time you need something. But that requires that we all cooperate in punishing you.

A simple solution is the emergence of the strong-man. The biggest and baddest in a group takes charge and enforces rules and agreements. It might still be inefficient because a lot has to be invested in maintaining dominance.

Slightly more advanced is an agreement among a group that someone is in charge.

Either solution presents administrative problems. It's hard to run a community without a staff. And it's hard for the staff to actually maintain order if it can't fall back on force and coercion. So the typical approach is to create a police force.

The first form of super intelligence that human intelligence created was the organization. Small groups of individuals pool efforts, cooperate, and coordinate to be able to accomplish tasks no individual could accomplish alone.

Hobbesian social contract has "the people" as principal and Leviathan (or state) as agent. Basic hierarchy idea is that social order requires some entity having power and authority over the rest.

"The State" is basically using an organization as the mechanism for maintaining order. But state coercion is perhaps ethically objectionable (difficult to specify social objective function correct) and economically it can be just too expensive (scalable oversight problem). Weber suggests force and coercion alone won't do it - states need to legitimacy - back to shared belief.

From Hechter and Horne (85): From an ethical view, as the nineteenth-century French anarchist Pierre-Joseph Proudhon noted,

To be GOVERNED is to be watched, inspected, spied upon, directed, law-driven, numbered, regulated, enrolled, indoctrinated, preached at, controlled, checked, estimated, valued, censured, commanded, by creatures who have neither the right nor the wisdom nor the virtue to do so. To be GOVERNED is to be at every operation, at every transaction noted, registered, counted, taxed, stamped, measured, numbered, assessed, licensed, authorized, admonished, prevented, forbidden, reformed, corrected, punished. It is, under pretext of public utility, and in the name of the general interest, to be placed under contribution, drilled, fleeced, exploited, monopolized, extorted from, squeezed, hoaxed, robbed; then, at the slightest resistance, the first word of complaint, to be repressed, fined, vilified, harrassed, hunted down, abused, clubbed, disarmed, bound, choked, imprisoned, judged, condemned, shot, deported, sacrificed, sold, betrayed; and to crown all, mocked, ridiculed, derided, outraged, dishonored. That is government; that is its justice; that is its morality. (Cited in Nozick 1974, 11)

Hierarchy can be described as top-down solutions to alignment and social order. Hierarchy addresses some of deficits of order via meaning but creates new problems. Expensive. Why would rational agents give up power? Submit to authority? Order might require oppression agents won't tolerate. And what about when putting someone in charge does not make sense? International relations. Groups of friends. Siblings.

One of our ways of conceptualizing social order is the capacity to cooperate to produce public goods.

Big deal is made of not having a conscious purpose. Not teleological. DJR: somewhat anti-humanist, to me. Hayek thinks he has a serious allergy to human vision and purpose.

Mostly Hayek wants government to just function as the maintenance department for society (coercive function of government). But he admits it should also provide services that won't be provided spontaneously.

Rules vs. commands. Rules allow use of knowledge which no individual possesses in the whole.151.1

Hayek is basically saying that you cannot specify the objective function by giving commands.

DJR: a lot of social science (at least mid 20th c) is investigation of how history and human science are compatible. Post NAZIsm, we try to understand how good people can be made to do bad things. Cold war requires us to mount intellectual defenses of capitalism and criticisms of socialism. Messiness of politics makes us try to develop mathematical versions of planning, decision-making, and such in hopes that we can take humans out of the loops. Failures of premature attempts to do that give rise to bounded rationality and wicked problems logics that suggest some upper limit created by complexity.

DJR: So we can make the jump, so to speak, by noting that our framework (human, organization, expert, machine) can be re-written in principal-agent terms. Any interaction between human intelligences is reciprocal agency: both participants are simultaneously principal and agent. Each wants the other to act or respond in certain ways. Each has private information, partially hidden actions, and the ability to reward, punish, and change the relationship. Organizations have a principal agent relationship with their members and society has a principal agent relationship with its organizations. Clients have a principal agent relationship with experts. Human–machine alignment is the design of machines as agents within human-governed institutions. The human, organization, or public is the principal; the model is the agent; reward models, evaluations, access controls, and deployment decisions form the “contract”; oversight is monitoring; and retraining, refusal, loss of access, or shutdown are sanctions or exit.
