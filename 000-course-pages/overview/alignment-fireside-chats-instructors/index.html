Instructions

Set up readings and 2 story comparison as pre-class work.  Classroom discussion draws on student work to fill in entire grid. We watch and read Russell's talk and add fifth column (after clarifying some vocabulary, perhaps). Then formulate discussion questions to be debated in groups.

Instructor Overview

Students read 2-4 short "talks" about alignment at various stages of human history (Alignment in the Beginning (instructors), Alignment and Neolithic Experts (instructors), Alignment c1620 (instructors),  Alignment and Cultural Transmission (instructors)). See Stuart Russell 2015 Value Alignment (5m18s).

These are all written by AI with guidance from Dan Ryan as parallels to Stuart Russell's 2015 WEF talk on "Stuart Russell 2015 Value Alignment (5m18s)"

Their task is to identify parallels and analogical elements. We scaffold the process with a set of questions as rows in a table with a column for each passage being compared.

  1. What is the opening question, and what does it define as "good"?
  2. What form does intelligence take here, and who holds it?
  3. Failure without a villain — how does catastrophe happen with no one wicked?
  4. But we need the eggs — why can't we just give the capability up?
  5. What mechanisms are proposed — and what does each not fix?

Here's the instructor's manual grid — five speeches across the top, five questions down the side, entries kept terse for at-a-glance use.

A — Hunter's Fire B — Merchants' Charter C — Expert/Knower D — Guild of Copyists Russell — Value Alignment
1. Opening question / "good" Good act = keeps you alive through all days ahead, not just tonight Good venture = greatest expected return over the enterprise's whole life Good choice = feeds your children over the whole turning of seasons Good teaching = knowledge stays true in unseen students' students Good decision = maximize expected utility over the whole future
2. Form of intelligence / who holds it General cleverness, distributed — everyone predicts, plans, remembers Concentrated in an artificial person — immortal, tireless corporation Concentrated in a few specialists — deep knowledge in fewer heads Concentrated in a lineage — knowledge transmitted master to student across generations Concentrated in the machine — optimization + perception, learned from data
3. Failure without a villain Rational mutual suspicion spirals into unwanted war — reasoning works perfectly Literal execution of an incomplete charter — diligence itself is the danger Untestable trust shifts to testing manner; expert drifts to serve the proxy Each master trims what he half-knows; error copies faithfully into doctrine Literal optimization of an underspecified objective — paperclips, the speeding car
4. Can't give up the capability Dulled band gets eaten by the undulled one — cleverness is competitive necessity No merchant abstains from ventures that yield fortunes — the charter stays, the risk must be managed Winter killed the band without specialists — expertise is what let others survive Knowledge untaught dies with its holder — plague years showed the cost of broken chains "Never happen" rebutted by Rutherford/Szilard; unlimited intelligence is coming regardless
5. Mechanisms and gaps Shared meals, public speech, gossip networks, dance, intermarriage — binds fate, doesn't read minds; hidden country stays hidden Read charter from custom/courts/sermons; ask Parliament before high-impact acts — assumes principal can still evaluate the request Mandatory teaching, disclose misses, prophesy before outcome, mouth ≠ hand — assumes an eventual external judge (the seasons) exists Independent rival lines, external examiners, return to sources, register departures, seal the record — nothing here handles a student who surpasses the master Inverse RL from the full human record, extended conversation before acting — assumes one machine, one human race in dialogue; silent on multiple AIs or AI-training-AI

Row 3 and row 5 are where the richest cross-speech discussion tends to land. Pairing "what breaks" with "what's proposed and what it doesn't fix" may be the fastest route to identify differences (symmetric peers / evaluable agent / unevaluable agent / unevaluable lineage).
