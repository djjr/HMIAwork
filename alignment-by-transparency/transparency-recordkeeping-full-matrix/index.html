Transparency & Recordkeeping — Full Mechanism × Domain Matrix

<<AI GENERATED + LIGHT EDITING BY DJR>> Companion to the HMIA 2025 "Transparency and Record Keeping" deck (slides.com/djjr/hmia2025-recordkeeping). The deck's grid slides (Records / Disclosures / Reporting requirements / Audits / Checklists, each crossed against Human Interaction / Organizational / Legal-Political / Professional-Expert / Machines) are placeholder ("LOREM," "xxx") everywhere except the Machines material already built out for model cards. This is a first-pass fill of the whole grid, plus interpretability and chain-of-thought (CoT) monitoring added to Machines as you asked — as two separate cells rather than one bundled entry, for reasons below.

Treat every cell as a draft to react to, not a finished answer — some are one clean example, some are three candidates you'll want to prune.

Framing recap (from the deck)

The deck's own claim: alignment among agents is obstructed by the opacity of inner states and of the world — you can't see what someone else is thinking, and even your own memory is fallible. Transparency and recordkeeping mechanisms counter that by manufacturing external traces of cognition and action that other people (or systems) can inspect, verify, and learn from. Records and disclosures make the past visible; reporting requirements and audits make ongoing behavior visible; checklists make the future — what's about to happen — visible and verifiable in advance.

The matrix

Mechanism Human Interaction Organizational Legal/Political Professional/Expert Machines
Records Diaries, letters, text/email threads — a private trace that outlives fallible memory (the deck's own hook question is basically this cell). Meeting minutes, internal memos; version-controlled commit history as an automatic, tamper-evident record of who changed what, when. Court transcripts, the legislative record; accumulating case law that later decisions must reckon with. Patient charts, lab notebooks; the line-infection tracking data that made Pronovost's intervention measurable in the first place (Gawande — deck resource). Training/eval logs; chain-of-thought traces — but a record only insofar as it's causally tied to the computation that produced the output, not just a plausible-sounding story after the fact (see faithfulness note below).
Disclosures Volunteering something material to a decision before the other person makes it. The AI-disclosure laws already in the deck (Utah, California — "the chatbot has to say"). Annual reports, published mission/values statements. Anthropic's own Constitution functions as an organizational disclosure of intended behavior (cf. ethics module). Financial disclosure by public officials; sunshine-law/FOIA default-disclosure regimes. Informed consent; disclosing compensation or conflicts of interest to a client (cf. ethics module's licensing-guild thread). Model cards (Mitchell et al. 2019 — the deck's anchor reading, "nutrition label" framing). Flag: system cards (already used in the deck's Claude 4 bias-evaluations dialog exercise) arguably belong more under Reporting requirements — periodic, covering safeguard results/agentic safety/alignment/welfare/reward hacking, not a one-time label.
Reporting requirements Mandatory-reporting laws — narrow cases where a standing legal obligation overrides personal discretion or confidentiality. Quarterly earnings, OSHA incident reporting; ongoing compliance reporting to a regulator on a fixed cadence. Agency reporting obligations to a legislature; treaty verification/reporting regimes. Adverse-event and malpractice reporting. This is the rationale the ethics module already gives for licensing-body oversight — a client can't continuously monitor an expert, so periodic reporting substitutes for direct oversight — the same shape as Amodei et al.'s scalable oversight. Responsible Scaling Policy commitments (the deck's own "Responsible Scaling" heading, from the Claude 4 system-card exercise); emerging frontier-model incident-reporting regimes; EU AI Act reporting obligations for high-risk systems.
Audits Fact-checking or double-checking someone else's claim informally. Financial audits; Ostrom's monitoring design principle for commons governance — graduated sanctions only work if someone is actually watching (cf. social-order reading list). Inspectors general, legislative investigations; the Shuttle Inquiry, already a deck resource. Peer review; licensing-board investigations. Mechanistic interpretability — the check on whether a CoT trace is actually faithful to the model's internal computation, rather than a plausible confabulation (Anthropic's circuit-tracing work / On the Biology of a Large Language Model, 2025). Also: red-teaming and bias evals (Claude 4 System Card exercise, already in deck); Metaxa et al. and Data & Society's Algorithmic Accountability, both already deck resources.
Checklists The Gawande material itself — an individual pilot's checklist habit as the founding case. Pronovost's central-line checklist and the Michigan Keystone rollout — the deck's central case study, an organization adopting a checklist as policy. Procedural requirements a bill must satisfy before passage; staged regulatory approval (e.g., FDA approval stages). Aviation's B-17-era pre-flight checklist (the origin story, already in Gawande); the WHO Surgical Safety Checklist as the direct medical-world extension. Pre-deployment evaluation gates tied to capability thresholds (RSP gating logic); automated test/eval suites a model release must pass before it ships.

Why interpretability and CoT are two cells, not one

CoT is the model's own externalized account of its reasoning — structurally that's a Record: a trace produced as a byproduct of the process, the same category as a meeting minute or a training log. Interpretability is someone else going into the mechanism to check whether that trace is true — structurally that's an Audit. The pairing only works as a transparency mechanism at all if the record is faithful to what's actually happening inside the model, and interpretability is currently one of the only tools that can check that. That's the same Records/Audits distinction already sitting elsewhere in this table (a patient chart vs. a peer-review audit of the physician), just imported into Machines. Worth making that structural point explicit to students rather than presenting the two as a matched pair of "new AI transparency stuff."

Other cross-cutting notes

  • Principal-agent bridge. The concrete-problems instructor notes already flag "objective function" as a bridge to the course's principal-agent thread (misaligned incentives, costly monitoring, bad behavior while learning). That's exactly what's driving the Reporting requirements row generally: reporting substitutes for continuous monitoring precisely when monitoring is the expensive part — the same shape as Scalable Oversight. Worth the explicit callback if this session comes after Concrete Problems.
  • Ethics-module bridge. The roommate/robot exercise's scaffolding move #4 — "a professional regime exists because the client can't fully monitor the expert" — is the Professional/Expert × Reporting-requirements cell in different words. A clean moment to have students notice the same sentence does double duty in two different modules.
  • Groups-strand tension (flagging, not resolving). Gossip and reputation — Dunbar's low-bandwidth reputation transmission, Coleman's closure1

Everyone talks and links overlap, bad behavior is exposed and punished by social disapproval without needing formal authorities.

Readings to add for the two new Machines cells

  • Turpin, Michael, Perez & Bowman, "Language Models Don't Always Say What They Think: Unfaithful Explanations in Chain-of-Thought Prompting)," NeurIPS 2023 — the CoT-unfaithfulness anchor; pairs well against the model-card "nutrition label" framing precisely because the two Machines-transparency artifacts behave so differently under scrutiny.
  • Lanham et al. (Anthropic), "Measuring Faithfulness in Chain-of-Thought Reasoning)," 2023 — companion, more measurement-oriented; good Pass-2-level skim per the how-to-read-a-paper guide.
  • Korbak, Balesni, Barnes, Bengio, Benton, Bloom, Chen, Cooney, Dafoe, Dragan et al., "Chain of Thought Monitorability: A New and Fragile Opportunity for AI Safety," 2025 — cross-lab position paper that frames CoT directly as an oversight/transparency mechanism and argues its usefulness is fragile. Strong discussion piece for the boundary between Reporting requirements and Audits.
  • Lindsey, Ameisen et al. (Anthropic), "Circuit Tracing: Revealing Computational Graphs in Language Models" and companion "On the Biology of a Large Language Model," 2025 (transformer-circuits.pub) — the Audits-row anchor. Heavy technical lift for the non-technical half of the audience; the "Tracing the Thoughts of a Large Language Model" blog post covers the same case studies at a much lighter level if that's the better fit given the AI 101 sequencing concerns already on file.
