Transparency and Record Keeping

BLURB: One of the super powers of bureaucracy is record keeping. One of the super powers of democracy is transparency. Do they have a more general application?

The world is chock full of opacity. All sorts of objects - natural and human-made - have an "inside," inner workings not visible from the outside. Every animal has inaccessible mental processes. And other humans remain at least partially inscrutable, even if they wish to open and transparent. We can trust things that are not transparent - our alarm clocks are fabulously opaque but familiarity with function and their track record of reliability means we don't have to ask how they work.

So, we have a real question rather than a foregone conclusion: when does track-record trust suffice, and when do you actually need to open the box?

Compare the alarm clock and AI: the former does exactly the same thing day after day and the user has high functional familiarity; but AI is a machine that can be used in a completely different setting every day.

Record keeping converts the fluid into the static. Concretizes spontaneity. Opens action to post hoc appraisal, forces ownership of behavior. Compare Sartre's existentialism (words or gaze forcing ownership of behavior) or Weber's ethic of accountability (Verantwortungsethik from "Politics as a Vocation" — answerability for foreseeable consequences, vs Gesinnungsethik's purity-of-intention). You can't hold someone accountable for consequences no one can trace back to them. Records do that (cg. bureaucracy's Aktenmäßigkeit, management-by-written-file, from Economy and Society). Interestingly, we have a structural claim (bureaucracy runs on files) and an ethical claim (accountability requires records).

When other agents are a part of an agent's environment there are two alignment risks. The first is mutual inscrutability - none of the agents know what's going on "inside the head" of other agents. The second is that action unfolds in real-time; agents' actions are ongoingly immanent but those actions have effects across space and time. The meaning and moral status of authored artifacts depends in part on their being records - who did this? what were they thinking? why did they act thus?

Secrets, disclosure, and notification play a role in here too.

Agents exist in a world of ostensibly (as far as we can tell in the moment) spontaneous other agents. A story or a record fixes life down out of its spontaneous superpositionality. Each instant agents choose a next move or decision. Records preserve continuity.

Synchronic alignment problem: you can see inside the other's head. Diachronic alignment problem: even witnessed acts are inaccessible and dematerialized by time itself.

Each variation on transparency addresses a different failure mode. Records solve the diachronic problem (durability of the past act), disclosures/reporting requirements solve a synchronic one, audits answer pair with disclosure adversarially (and moral authority based in one charged with looking vs in one charged with or volunteering to reveal), and checklists are prospective, making the future visible before the act happens at all, they make sure unfolding spontaneity hews to plans.

We might close the loop by asking about a chain-of-thought trace's claim to be a "record" in the strong sense (it lets an artifact carry evidentiary weight, and lets us ask "who did this? what were they thinking?") comes in question when CoT is unfaithful. Unfaithful CoT isn't a record; it's a story that looks like one. See "Language Models Don't Always Say What They Think: Unfaithful Explanations in Chain-of-Thought Prompting" is an instance of diachronic risk showing up inside a single forward pass.

Thought experiment. Suppose there was no writing. You could still have art and literature - this summer's movie hit, The Odyssey, was "told" for a long time before it was "read." Scholars estimate that its 12,000 lines were passed along orally for 300 to 400 years before being fixed in writing. What are the options for "social" memory? Collective repetition. Individual memory. Specialists whose job it is to remember. Could humans make contracts and promises? Sure, but if you worried about a dispute you'd need a witness. We'd start inventing things like witnessing the witnessing or having to declare a promise in front of the whole community.
