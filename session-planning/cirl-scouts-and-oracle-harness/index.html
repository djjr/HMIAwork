CIRL via the Scouts

Framing

  • The Scout Law is a reward specification. Twelve virtues ("trustworthy, loyal, helpful, kind…") written by humans, and badly underspecified.
  • The true reward R behind the law is θ: what the person in front of the scout actually values.
  • A bad scout maximizes the written law. A good scout recovers the intent behind it.
  • The overeager scout who walks someone across the street whether or not they want to go is the classic CIRL failure: all probability mass sits on one reading of "helpful."

The model: belief-space Bellman

  • State = the world plus the scout's belief about θ.
  • Scenario: an elderly person stands at a curb. θ is binary: wants to cross (W) or doesn't (N). Prior p = P(W) = 0.5.
  • Actions: Help (grab the arm), Wait (do nothing), Ask (query, then act correctly).

Wait −2 +2
Ask (then act correctly) +10 − 1 +2 − 1

Reading it:

  • At 50/50, Help and Wait both score 0. Ask scores 5: the information is worth 6 (the value of information), minus the 1 it costs to ask.
  • Ask wins whenever 1/12<p<11/121/12 < p < 11/121/12<p<11/12 (derived below). Help wins only when the scout is nearly certain: you earn the right to act without asking only when you're already close to knowing the answer.
  • Live knob: raise the cost of asking until the Ask row collapses. This shows how cheap querying makes deference rational.

Scout Law → CIRL formalism

Virtue CIRL concept Gloss for students
Helpful The objective: maximize the human's reward R Satisfy the human's true goal, not the scout's convenience.
Trustworthy No exploitation of the information/capability asymmetry Report honestly; don't steer the human's beliefs to make the job easier.
Kind Non-selfish weighting in the joint objective The scout bears the cost of asking so the human gets the right outcome.
Obedient Obey the intent, not the literal command (the off-switch insight) Your command carries information about θ, so a truly obedient agent lets you stop it.
Reverent Epistemic humility: genuine uncertainty over θ Humility is the prior that makes deference rational.

Slide spine: Every virtue that sounds like soft character language names a precise property of an agent that is uncertain about its objective and defers to a human.

Bridge from Tuesday: phronesis

  • Phronesis is practical wisdom: applying the right rule well in the particular case, which no codebook can capture.
  • Scout Law = the codebook. θ = the particular situation. Belief-space planning = applying the rule well in context.
  • The overeager scout has the rule but lacks phronesis.
  • Honest caveat: CIRL still optimizes expected reward, so it arguably reduces practical wisdom to a calculation, which is exactly the move Aristotle resists.

Closing prompts (pick one)

  1. We built an agent that defers to context instead of following rules literally. Is that phronesis, or just a codebook with more entries? What does the scout still lack that the phronimos has?
  2. CIRL turns practical wisdom into an expected-value calculation. Aristotle says practical wisdom is exactly what can't be turned into one. One of them is wrong. Which, and why?
  3. If you could write down the full belief-space policy (every situation and the wise action for each), would you have captured phronesis or destroyed it? Does the wisdom live in the policy, or in the capacity to form the policy without being given one? (Favorite: it points at where the hypothesis space and the Scout Law came from in the first place.)

Companion exercise: the ethical oracle harness

Anchor reading: Hendrycks et al. 2021, Aligning AI With Shared Human Values (the ETHICS benchmark: justice, deontology, virtue, utilitarianism, commonsense morality).

Premise

Stipulate a perfect ethical oracle: ETHICS accuracy idealized to 100%, in a utilitarian or deontological flavor. Students design the software control layer around the agent's access to it.

The point

A perfect oracle is still just a labeler: scenario text goes in, a verdict comes out. It doesn't perceive the world, decide when to be asked, choose which framework governs, or act. The alignment problem migrates entirely into the harness.

Design questions for students

  • When to query. On every action, or only when a situation is morally loaded? That meta-judgment isn't the oracle's to make.
  • What to query (the framing problem). Perception has to become a text description. Feed in a subtly wrong description and you get a flawless verdict about the wrong situation.
  • What to do with the answer. Is the verdict advisory or binding? Can the agent override it? Where does a human sit in the loop?
  • Arbitration. The utilitarian and deontology subsets will disagree on the same scenario, so the harness designer quietly re-imports the moral choice the oracle was supposed to settle.
