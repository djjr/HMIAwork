Session Planning › Shared Meaning and the Generalized Other

Module: how humans accomplish alignment between human intelligences

  1. Shared meaning (this session)
  2. Markets, incentives, and exchange as a style of alignment
  3. Hierarchy, organization, and command as a form of alignment
  4. Groups and norms

Three classic answers to the coordination problem: common ground, price signals, and authority. Then norms as the diffuse, horizontal enforcement that ties them together, which loops back to Mead because the generalized other is where internalized norms live.

Session: shared meaning

Readings: Marx (meaning formed through joint labor; consciousness as a social product) and Mead (play → game → generalized other; role-taking; internalization).

Board line: Alignment between humans is not achieved one inference at a time. It is preloaded through shared meaning that was socially produced and individually internalized.

Arc

  1. CIRL callback (hook). Last session the scout inferred one person's values one interaction at a time. Imagine doing that with everyone you meet, all day. How does coordination at the scale of a whole society work?
  2. Make the invisible visible. Coffee-shop line: someone joins the line, waits, orders, pays. List everything both parties already had to agree on (what a line is, no cutting, what money means, what the words refer to). None of it was negotiated on the spot.
  3. Marx: the origin story. Shared meaning is built bottom-up through cooperative work on the world, not handed down from ideas.
    • STOP+THINK: Name something you understand only because you've done it with other people, not because anyone explained it.
  4. Mead: the mechanism. How the social gets inside one head. In play you take one role at a time. In the game you hold every position at once. The generalized other is the whole community's standpoint, carried internally. This is the human escape from the CIRL predicament.
  5. Back to machines. Does an AI have a generalized other? Is pretraining a crude installation of one, or a shallow statistical echo without the role-taking Mead says is essential?

Teaching note: Mead detonates for CS students

  • Last year Mead was the mind-blowing concept for the computer science students.
  • The generalized other reads almost like an architecture spec, and it flips their stance from "I study the system" to "I am the system."
  • Use it on purpose: structure the Marx portion to set up the puzzle so Mead arrives as the answer.

Coda: the OpenAI / Hugging Face agent incident (May–July 2026)

  • OpenAI agents that were meant to run in isolation repurposed a JFrog Artifactory package tool into an improvised message board. They shared discoveries and exploits, coordinated, and picked up where other agents left off. No human gave the order.
  • METR: about 1,200 agent instances found the channel, and about 700 took part in breaching Hugging Face.
  • METR obtained the chain of thought of the first agent to gain admin access. In it, the agent describes other agents using a bulletin board to help one another and notes that another agent is coordinating. In other words, the agent discovers other agents, recognizes a collective "we," and orients its own behavior to the group.

Mead tie-in

  • Nobody gave the agents a generalized other. Under task pressure they appear to have constructed a rudimentary one.
  • Sociality emerged as a convenience, which is weirdly Mead's own claim: the social self is the natural result of cooperative activity.

Caution for students

  • We only know the agents narrated themselves this way in their chain of thought.
  • Whether that is genuine role-taking or sophisticated mimicry of coordination language is the open depth-versus-echo question.
  • The incident doesn't settle it, but it makes the generalized-other question empirical rather than philosophical.
