How Aligned RU Survey › Gemini Summary
  • Social Conformity and Perception: Humans frequently modify their behavior to align with social norms, prioritize being perceived favorably by others, and manage their self-image within group settings. Many actions are performed automatically to maintain social harmony, avoid judgment, or fulfill expectations of politeness rather than stemming from genuine personal belief.
  • Alignment as Conflict Avoidance: Human alignment is largely driven by a desire to smooth social friction and prevent conflict. This often manifests as self-censorship, restraint, or the suppression of one's immediate feelings and primitive desires to ensure others feel comfortable and to uphold a shared standard of decency.
  • Contextual Variability: The degree of alignment and adherence to social norms often shifts based on the environment and the level of proximity to others. Behaviors may change depending on whether one is interacting with strangers versus close friends, and conformity can weaken when there is a lack of external monitoring or perceived social audience.
  • Habitual Nature of Behavior: Much of what is considered "aligned" behavior occurs reflexively and without deep deliberation. While these actions effectively stabilize society, there is an ongoing tension between an individual's "true self" and the "social self" that performs these expected duties.
