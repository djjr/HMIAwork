Supervised Fine-Tuning, in plain language

After pretraining, you have a model that's very good at one narrow thing: continuing text in whatever direction the internet would naturally continue it. Ask it a question, and it might just as easily continue with more questions, or wander off into unrelated text, because that's what "plausible internet continuation" often looks like. It has no notion that it's supposed to be answering you.

Supervised fine-tuning is the step where we teach it that new job. We build a dataset that's different in kind from the internet corpus — instead of raw text, it's made of matched pairs: a prompt on one side ("Summarize this article...") and a good, human-quality response on the other ("The article argues that..."). These pairs are meant to model the behavior of a helpful assistant, not just grammatical English.

Then we do something that's mechanically the same as pretraining, just pointed at this new data: for each word (token) in the response, the model is asked to predict it, given the prompt and everything in the response so far. It gets scored on how close its guess was to the actual next token, and that score becomes a signal for nudging the model's internal settings.

Here's the important shift in what that nudging is teaching, even though the training mechanism looks identical to pretraining: in pretraining, the model is learning "what tends to come next on the internet." In SFT, it's learning "what a helpful assistant tends to say next, given a prompt and its own response so far." Same mechanical process — predict the next token, check how wrong you were, adjust — but a differently curated dataset shapes it toward the new behavior.

The net effect: after SFT, the model has developed a propensity toward producing the kind of responses a helpful assistant would give — answering questions, following instructions, staying on topic — rather than simply continuing text in whatever direction raw internet data would drift. It's not yet optimized for what humans actually prefer among possible good responses (that's the next stage, using human preference comparisons) — it's just learned the basic shape of "answer, don't just continue."
